Concept project · Interface

Glass UI

How can translucent layers add atmosphere without making an interface harder to read?

Type
Interface design concept
Year
2026
Role
Independent design and front-end exploration
Tools
HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript
Deliverables
Responsive page, glass-surface styles, interaction prototype
The question

Atmosphere needs hierarchy.

Glassmorphism can create depth, but stacking translucent surfaces over detailed imagery can reduce contrast and blur the visual order. This concept explores a calmer balance between a distinctive mood and readable content.

Goals & constraints

  • Keep text and controls clearly separated from decorative imagery.
  • Use blur and transparency as accents rather than applying them everywhere.
  • Keep the design responsive and implementable with native CSS.
  • Respect reduced-motion preferences and keyboard interaction.

Design exploration

Three directions were considered: a highly transparent panel, a more opaque card, and a restrained layered surface. The strongest direction uses an opaque-enough content panel with a subtle border, a darker image overlay, and one accent color. These are design alternatives, not a record of formal user testing.

Final direction & decisions

The concept keeps the visual texture behind the content while treating the text panel as the primary reading surface. Blur is limited to selected panels, borders define edges, and spacing—not glow—does most of the hierarchy work.

What I learned

Transparency is not a substitute for contrast. A useful next step would be checking text contrast across more background crops and testing the panel with longer, realistic content.
